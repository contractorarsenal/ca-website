What It Is

A free listing Google controls,
and a website you own.

A Google Business Profile is the box with your name, phone number, hours, reviews, and photos that shows in Google Search and Maps. It is not your website, and it does not replace one.

Your Google profile

  • Shows in the map results
  • Reviews, hours, and a call button at a glance
  • Lives on Google, under Google's rules
  • Short by design, with no room for detail

Your website

  • Explains each service in depth
  • Shows your real project work
  • Yours to control, with no outside rules on layout
  • Where the customer decides to call
The Problem

Set up once. Never looked at again.

Most contractor profiles were created years ago and left alone. Meanwhile the services changed, the phone number changed, or the website now says something different. Google and customers both notice when the two disagree.

  • The profile lists services the website never mentions, or the other way around.
  • A broad category like "Contractor" where a specific one would fit.
  • A home address on display for a business that goes to its customers.
  • Old hours, an old phone number, or reviews nobody replied to.

01

Category

The primary category tells Google what kind of business you are. Google says to use as few as possible and pick the most specific one that is true.

02

Services

The services on your profile should use the same names as the service pages on your website.

03

Service areas

If you go to the customer, you list the cities or postal codes you serve instead of showing an address. Up to 20 areas.

04

Reviews

Google counts reviews toward how well known you are. Ask after every good job and reply to each one in your own words.

05

Photos

Real photos of your crew and finished work. Homeowners can tell stock photos from the real thing.

06

Consistency

Name, phone, hours, and services should read the same on your profile and on your site.

How Google picks who shows up locally

Google says local results come down mainly to three things: relevance (do you match the search), distance (how close you are to the person searching), and prominence (how well known you are, including reviews). It also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, so no one can promise you a spot on the map.

Common Mistakes

What gets a profile ignored, or suspended.

These come straight from Google's guidelines for representing a business. Most are attempts to look bigger or closer than you are.

  • Keywords or a city in the business name. The name should be your real-world name, as it appears on your trucks and paperwork.
  • A virtual office or P.O. box as the address. These are not eligible.
  • More than one profile for the same business. A service-area business gets one profile for its whole area.
  • Using categories as keywords. Categories describe what the business is, not everything it offers.
  • Leaving verification unfinished. An unverified profile cannot be edited or managed.
