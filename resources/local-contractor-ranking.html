How Google Decides Which Contractor Shows Up Locally

There's no secret trick. Google is trying to answer one question: which business best matches what this person needs, right now, nearby.

Google won't tell anyone the exact formula, and no one legitimately knows all of it. But the pieces that matter are well understood, and none of them are secret. They're just easy to skip.

What Google Says It Uses

Google's own Business Profile help says local results are based mainly on three things: relevance, distance, and prominence. That is the map results and the box of three businesses that shows above the regular links. You can read it in Google's guide to local ranking.

  • Relevance: how well your business matches what the person searched for
  • Distance: how far you are from the person searching, or from the place they named
  • Prominence: how well known your business is, which Google ties to reviews and to how many other websites link to you

Everything else in this guide is one of those three, looked at from a contractor's side.

Relevance

Does your business clearly match what the person searched? If someone searches "emergency plumber Tacoma" and your site talks generally about "quality plumbing services" with no mention of emergency calls or Tacoma, that's a weak match, no matter how good your work actually is.

Relevance comes from two places you control. On your Google Business Profile, it is your category, your list of services, and how complete the profile is. On your website, it is whether a real page exists for the job being searched. A roofer with a page about roof repair is an easier match for "roof repair" than a roofer whose homepage mentions it once.

Distance and Location

Distance is the factor you have the least control over. Google looks at where the searcher is. A homeowner in Tacoma and a homeowner in Everett typing the same words will see different contractors, and that is by design.

What you can control is whether Google understands where you work. Most contractors travel to the customer, which Google calls a service-area business. Google's rules for that setup are specific:

  • If you don't serve customers at your business address, remove the address from your profile and list the areas you serve instead
  • Service areas are set by city, postal code, or similar area. You cannot set a radius
  • You can list up to 20 service areas
  • The whole area should stay within about two hours of driving from where you are based

Those come from Google's service-area business help page. On your website, the matching piece is clear service-area content that says where you work in plain words.

Google Business

Is the profile verified, complete, and consistent? Google lists this first in its own tips: verify the business, then keep the information complete and current, including hours, category, and how to reach you. A profile that's half-filled out, or contradicts your site, works against you. Our guide to optimizing your Google Business Profile goes through each part.

Reviews

Do customers trust the company? Google names review count and rating as part of prominence, and they're one of the first things a customer looks at too. Two of Google's own tips are about reviews: get more of them, and reply to the ones you have. Ask happy customers shortly after the job is done. Reply to every review, good or bad, in your own words.

Website

Does the website support the same information? Services, service area, and business details should be consistent and clearly written, with a page dedicated to each real service. This is the part you fully own, and it is where the detail lives that a profile has no room for. See why each service needs its own page.

Authority

Does Google have enough evidence that the company is legitimate and useful? Google says prominence also depends on how many websites link to the business. For a contractor that usually means ordinary, real mentions: a supplier's dealer list, a local chamber or trade association, a manufacturer certification page, a local news story. This builds slowly, and there is no honest shortcut.

Why a Competitor Shows Up Above You

When another contractor sits above you for a search, it is almost always one of these:

  • They are closer to the person searching. You can't change this. Search from a different part of town and the order often changes
  • Their category or services match the search more exactly. You can fix this in your profile and on your site
  • They have more reviews, or more recent ones. You can work on this every week
  • Their profile is more complete. Hours, services, photos, and a description all filled in
  • They have been around longer and more local sites mention them. This takes time

Check from the customer's side before you worry. Results you see from your own office are tilted toward your own location.

What You Cannot Buy

Google states it directly: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." Ads are a separate thing, and they are labeled as ads. Anyone selling a guaranteed spot in the map results is selling something Google says does not exist.

What to Do This Week

  • Confirm your profile is verified and your category is the most specific one that fits
  • Check that your service areas are set by city or postal code and match what your website says
  • Make sure your name, phone number, and hours are identical on your profile and your site
  • Ask your last three happy customers for a review, and reply to any review you have not answered
  • List the services people actually search for and check that each one has its own page

None of this guarantees a specific ranking, and no one who tells you otherwise is being straight with you. What it does is give you a real foundation to compete on, instead of hoping.
