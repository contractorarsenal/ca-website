Why Cheap Contractor Websites Usually Stop Working

The lowest upfront price rarely stays the cheapest option. Here's what usually happens six months in.

Contractor ArsenalUpdated August 2026

The Real Cost Isn't the Sticker Price

A $200 website and a $297-a-month managed website aren't really the same purchase. One is a one-time build. The other is a website plus everything that keeps a website working: hosting, updates, SEO structure, and someone to call when it breaks.

The cheap option usually looks like the better deal until you need something changed and find out that costs extra, takes two weeks, or requires you to learn a builder tool yourself.

Templates Run Out of Room

Most low-cost builds start from a generic template that isn't built around your trade, your services, or your city. That's fine for a single homepage. It falls apart the moment you need a dedicated page for a specific service, or a second location, or a real project gallery.

A template can hold one page well. It struggles to hold a structure: separate service pages, city pages, and a project archive that actually helps you rank.

Who Handles It When Something Breaks

Websites go down. Plugins conflict. A form stops sending leads to your inbox and nobody notices for three weeks. With a cheap, self-managed build, that's your problem to diagnose. With a managed website, it's someone else's job to catch it before you do.

Hosting and Support Add Up

Hosting, SSL, backups, and basic maintenance aren't free even on the cheapest platforms. Add them up separately and the "cheap" website often costs close to what a fully managed one would, minus the SEO structure, the copywriting, and the support.

  • Hosting and SSL renewal
  • Time spent making your own updates
  • Add-on costs for forms, speed, or backups
  • The cost of leads lost while something was broken and nobody knew

Questions to Ask Before You Buy

  • Who updates the site when my services or prices change
  • What happens to my site and domain if I want to leave
  • Is local SEO structure included, or a separate cost
  • What's the actual response time if something breaks
  • Do I own the content, or does it belong to the platform

None of these questions have a wrong answer. They just need an honest one before you commit.
