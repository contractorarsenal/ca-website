The Painting Contractor Website Checklist

Painting is a visual trade, and a painting website that doesn't show real work is asking a homeowner to trust it on faith alone.

What Makes Painting Websites Different

Painting is one of the most visual trades a contractor can be in. A homeowner deciding whether to hire you is really deciding whether they trust your work to look good on their own house, and the single best way to earn that trust is showing them actual finished work, not describing it.

That changes what a painting website should prioritize compared to other trades. Photos aren't a nice-to-have here, they're close to the whole pitch.

Service Pages You Should Have

Split your services into pages that match how homeowners actually search:

  • Interior painting, since this is usually the most searched and most requested service
  • Exterior painting, which is a different job with different weather and prep considerations worth calling out separately
  • Cabinet refinishing or commercial painting, if either is a real part of your business
  • Color consultation, mentioned as a value-add if you offer it. It's a small detail that can differentiate you from a painter who just shows up and paints whatever's picked

What to Show for Trust

Real before/after project photos are the single most important trust signal for a painting business, more than almost anything else on the page. Reviews matter too, and licensing or insurance information should be stated clearly where your state or city requires it.

  • Real before/after photos, front and center, not buried at the bottom of the page
  • Reviews that mention specific completed jobs
  • Licensing and insurance details where applicable to your area

Before/After Photos Done Right

Not all before/after photos are created equal. The details matter as much as having them at all.

Skip: stock photography, mismatched lighting between the before and after shot, or photos with no context.

Use: real side-by-side photos from actual jobs, captioned with the project type and general location, so a visitor can tell it's genuine local work.

Lead Capture for Painting Specifically

Keep the estimate form simple. Painting estimates often depend on details a homeowner can answer in a sentence or two, so don't demand a full project breakdown before they've even decided to reach out.

  • Ask generally about rooms or square footage and interior versus exterior, without requiring exact measurements upfront
  • Keep the form short enough that someone can fill it out from their phone in under a minute
  • Make click-to-call prominent for homeowners who'd rather just describe the job over the phone

Quick Checklist

A fast recap to run your own painting site against:

  • Separate pages for interior and exterior painting
  • A cabinet refinishing or commercial page if that's part of your business
  • Color consultation mentioned as a value-add, if offered
  • Real before/after photos placed prominently, not buried
  • Before/after photos captioned with project type and location
  • No stock photography standing in for real project photos
  • Reviews that reference specific completed jobs
  • Licensing and insurance information where applicable
  • A short estimate form asking rooms, square footage, and interior versus exterior
  • Prominent, tappable click-to-call on every page
