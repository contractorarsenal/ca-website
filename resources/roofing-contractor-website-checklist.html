The Roofing Contractor Website Checklist

Roofing leads are often urgent and often expensive to earn. A roofing website needs to work harder than a generic contractor site to turn that urgency into a call.

Why Roofing Websites Need Their Own Standard

Roofing customers behave differently than most other trades. A lot of roofing inquiries start with a problem that already exists, a leak, storm damage, a failed inspection before a home sale, and the customer wants an answer fast. A generic contractor website template doesn't account for that urgency.

A roofing site needs to be organized around the actual questions a homeowner is asking, not a generic "our services" page that lumps everything together.

Service Pages You Should Have

Separate pages perform better than one page trying to cover everything, because each page can speak directly to a specific problem:

  • Roof replacement, for homeowners planning a full re-roof
  • Roof repair, for smaller, more urgent fixes
  • Material-specific pages where relevant, shingle, metal, flat, or commercial roofing, if those are meaningfully different services for your business
  • Storm damage and insurance claim assistance, since this is often a completely different search and mindset than a routine repair

For a deeper look at why splitting services into their own pages helps, see our guide on roofing service page structure.

What to Show for Trust

Roofing is a big-ticket, high-stakes job for most homeowners. Trust signals matter more here than on almost any other trade page.

  • Licensing and insurance information, stated clearly, not buried in fine print
  • Manufacturer certifications where applicable, since these can matter for warranty coverage
  • Real project photos of actual completed roofs, not stock imagery of generic houses
  • Roofing-specific reviews, ideally ones that mention the type of work performed

Storm and Emergency Response

After a storm, search volume for roofing repair spikes fast, and homeowners are often comparing several companies within the same hour. Your site needs to communicate urgency and reachability immediately.

  • Fast-response messaging near the top of the page, not buried below a long intro
  • A clear, visible phone number that works on mobile without zooming or scrolling
  • Consider a dedicated storm-damage or emergency-leak page, since these are genuinely urgent, high-intent searches that deserve their own landing experience

Lead Capture for Roofing Specifically

A generic contact form loses roofing leads. Ask for the details that actually help you triage the job:

  • An estimate form that asks about roof type and the type of issue, leak, storm damage, full replacement
  • Prominent click-to-call, since roofing leads skew urgent and many homeowners will call instead of filling out a form if calling is easy
  • Clear expectations about callback timing. You don't need to promise an exact number of hours, just be upfront about when someone will actually respond

Quick Checklist

A fast recap to run your own roofing site against:

  • Separate pages for roof replacement and roof repair
  • A dedicated storm damage or insurance claim page
  • Material-specific pages if your business genuinely differentiates on material
  • Licensing and insurance information clearly stated
  • Manufacturer certifications listed where applicable
  • Real project photos, not stock imagery
  • Roofing-specific reviews visible on the site
  • A visible, tappable phone number on every page
  • An estimate form that asks for roof type and issue type
  • Clear, honest expectations for callback timing
