Should Your Contractor Business Run Google Ads?

Google Ads can bring in fast leads or burn through a budget with nothing to show for it. Which one happens depends mostly on what's already true about your website.

What Google Ads Actually Does

Google Ads is paid placement. You bid on keywords, write an ad, and pay each time someone clicks it, regardless of whether that click turns into a job. It's separate from organic SEO, which is free to appear in but takes time to build and can't be turned on overnight.

The appeal is speed. A new campaign can start generating clicks the same day it launches. The tradeoff is that every one of those clicks costs money, whether or not the visitor ever calls.

When Ads Make Sense

  • You need leads now and can't wait for organic search rankings to build over months
  • You have the crew capacity to actually handle a sudden jump in lead volume, not just the marketing budget to generate it
  • You have a fast-loading page with a clear call-to-action ready to receive that paid traffic, not just your general homepage

When Ads Are a Waste of Money

  • Sending paid clicks to a slow website or a page that doesn't clearly say what you do
  • Having no fast follow-up process, so leads sit for hours or days before anyone calls them back
  • Competing on a keyword without enough budget to gather real data on what's actually working

Ads amplify whatever is already true about your website. A slow, unclear site just loses money faster once paid traffic is pointed at it.

What Has to Be True on Your Website First

Before spending on ads, a few things need to already be in place:

  • A visible phone number and clear call-to-action above the fold, especially on mobile
  • Real page speed. A slow-loading landing page burns ad spend before the visitor even sees your offer
  • A landing page that matches what was actually searched, not a generic homepage that makes the visitor hunt for relevance

Budget Reality

Cost-per-click varies significantly depending on your trade and your city, and we're not going to hand you a made-up number here. Treat ad spend as something to test and measure, not a fixed cost you commit to blindly.

Measure results by calls and leads generated, not by clicks or impressions. Clicks are easy to buy. Calls and booked jobs are what actually matter, and that's the number to watch as you decide whether to keep spending, adjust, or pull back.

Ads vs. Organic: Not Either/Or

Most established contractors eventually run both. Organic search and Google Business Profile visibility build steadily over time and don't stop working when you pause spending. Ads deliver quick volume when you need it, but stop the moment you stop paying.

They're not substitutes for each other. A healthy long-term approach usually leans on organic visibility as the foundation and uses ads deliberately, when there's capacity to handle the leads and a website ready to convert them.
