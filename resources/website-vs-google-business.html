Website vs. Google Business Profile: Why You Need Both

They're not competing for the same job. Used together, they cover far more ground than either one alone.

Contractors sometimes ask if they even need a website once their Google Business Profile is set up. The honest answer: they do two different jobs, and a customer usually touches both before they call.

What Google Business Is Good For

Google Business

  • Showing up on Maps
  • Displaying reviews at a glance
  • Quick calls straight from search results
  • Directions
  • Fast local visibility

Your Website

  • Explaining your services in detail
  • Showing real project work
  • Longer-form trust and information
  • Dedicated SEO pages per service
  • Lead forms and content you fully own

The profile is what shows in the map results. Google says those results come down to relevance, distance, and prominence, and a verified, complete profile is the starting point for all three.

What Your Website Is Good For

Google Business is fast and visual, but shallow by design. It's not built to explain the difference between a roof repair and a full replacement, or to show ten photos of a finished kitchen. That depth lives on your website, where you control the structure and the story.

A website is also where a customer decides. Someone comparing a few contractors opens each site to check the same things: do they do my kind of job, do they work in my area, does the work look good, and how do I reach them. A profile can't answer all of that. A page for each service can.

Who Controls Each One

This is the difference most contractors miss. Your profile lives on Google's platform, under Google's rules. Google decides how you verify, reviews some edits before they show, and can suspend a profile that breaks its guidelines. Customers and Google can suggest changes to it.

Your website is yours. Nobody else edits it, the layout is your choice, and it does not disappear because a rule changed. That is the reason not to build your whole online presence on a profile alone.

How They Work Together

A customer often finds you on Maps first, then clicks through to your website to confirm the details before calling. If the website doesn't exist, or contradicts the profile, that customer hesitates.

It runs the other way too. Google says prominence is partly based on what it can find about a business across the web. A real website with clear service and service-area pages gives Google more to work with than a profile by itself.

Think of Google Business as the front door and your website as the rest of the house. One gets someone to stop by. The other convinces them to stay.

What Has to Match

Go through both side by side once a quarter. These should read the same in both places:

  • Business name, exactly as it appears on your trucks and paperwork
  • Phone number
  • Hours
  • The cities or areas you serve
  • Your list of services, using the same names
  • The website link on the profile, pointing at a page that works

If You Only Have One

Profile but no website: you can show up on Maps, but a customer who wants to know more has nowhere to go, and you have nothing that explains your services in depth.

Website but no profile: you can explain everything, but you are missing from the map results, which is where a lot of "near me" searches end.

If you're starting from zero, do the profile first because it is free and quick, then build the website it points to. Our guide to setting up the profile properly covers the first half.
