How to Optimize Your Google Business Profile as a Contractor

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a homeowner sees, before they ever reach your website. Here's what actually matters in it.

Why Your Profile Matters as Much as Your Website

A lot of contractors treat their Google Business Profile as an afterthought, something they set up once and never touch again. But for local searches, it often shows up before your website does, complete with your phone number, reviews, hours, and photos, right there on the results page.

A weak or outdated profile can undo a good website. A strong one can send you calls even on days your website gets no traffic at all.

Getting the Basics Right

Start with the fundamentals, because small mistakes here cause real problems later.

  • Use your actual, legal business name. No stuffing extra keywords like "Best Roofing Seattle" into the name field
  • Keep your phone number and hours current. Nothing frustrates a homeowner faster than calling a number that doesn't work
  • Set up service-area coverage correctly if you don't have a public storefront customers walk into. Most contractors should hide their exact address and list a service area instead

Categories and Services

Your primary category should be the most accurate match for your main trade. Add relevant secondary categories, but don't pile on categories that don't apply just to appear in more searches.

  • Choose one primary category that matches your core business
  • Add secondary categories only where they're genuinely accurate
  • Build out your services list to match the services listed on your actual website, word for word where possible

Photos That Actually Help

Real project photos do more work here than almost anything else on the profile. A homeowner scrolling through profiles can tell the difference between stock photography and an actual job you completed.

Skip: stock photos of generic tools, generic houses, or smiling models in hard hats.

Use: photos of your own completed jobs, your crew on-site, and before/after shots when the work allows for it.

Reviews and Responses

Reviews are one of the biggest trust signals on the entire profile, and how you respond to them matters almost as much as the reviews themselves.

  • Ask for a review shortly after a job wraps up, while the experience is still fresh
  • Respond to positive reviews with a short, genuine thank you, not a copy-pasted script
  • Respond to negative reviews calmly and professionally. How you handle a bad review often matters more to future customers than the review itself

Keeping It Updated

A profile that hasn't been touched in a year looks abandoned, even if your business is thriving. Keep hours accurate, especially around holidays, and keep your phone number current if it ever changes.

Posts and updates can help, but use them sparingly and with a purpose, a completed project, a seasonal service reminder, not filler content posted just to post something. A profile that's accurate and occasionally updated will outperform one that's either stale or spammed with noise.
