How to Optimize Your Google Business Profile as a Contractor

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing a homeowner sees, before they ever reach your website. Here's what actually matters in it.

Why Your Profile Matters as Much as Your Website

A lot of contractors treat their Google Business Profile as an afterthought, something they set up once and never touch again. But for local searches, it often shows up before your website does, complete with your phone number, reviews, hours, and photos, right there on the results page.

A weak or outdated profile can undo a good website. A strong one can send you calls even on days your website gets no traffic at all.

Get Verified First

Nothing else on this list matters until the profile is verified. Verification is how Google confirms you are allowed to represent the business, and it is what lets you edit the profile and reply to customers.

  • Google decides which verification methods you are offered. You can't pick
  • The options are a phone call or text, an email, a video recording, a live video call, or a postcard in the mail
  • If you are offered video recording, Google recommends using it
  • Postcard codes usually arrive within 14 days. Don't change your name, address, or category while you wait, and don't request a second code
  • After you submit, Google's review can take up to five business days

Details are on Google's verification help page.

Getting the Basics Right

Start with the fundamentals, because small mistakes here cause real problems later.

  • Use your real business name, the way it appears on your trucks, your invoices, and your website. Adding a city or a service to the name field, like "Best Roofing Seattle," breaks Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended
  • Use a local phone number that your business controls, and keep it and your hours current. Nothing frustrates a homeowner faster than calling a number that doesn't work
  • Write a plain description of what you do and where. Google does not allow links in the description, and it is not the place for prices or promotions

Service Area and Address

Most contractors go to the customer. Google calls that a service-area business, and the setup is different from a shop with a front door.

  • If customers don't come to your address, remove the address from the profile. A contractor running the business from home should not show the home address
  • List the areas you serve by city or postal code. You can't draw a radius
  • You can add up to 20 service areas
  • Keep the whole area within about two hours of driving from where you are based
  • A service-area business gets one profile for its whole area, not one per city

P.O. boxes and rented virtual office addresses are not eligible. The rules are on Google's service-area business page.

Categories and Services

Your primary category should be the most specific accurate match for your main trade. Google's own wording is to use as few categories as possible, and to pick ones that finish the sentence "This business is a," not "This business has a."

  • Choose one primary category that matches your core business. "Roofing contractor" beats "Contractor" if roofing is what you do
  • Add secondary categories only where they're genuinely accurate
  • Don't use categories as a place for extra keywords
  • Build out your services list to match the services listed on your actual website, word for word where possible

Photos That Actually Help

Real project photos do more work here than almost anything else on the profile. Adding photos is one of the tips Google itself gives for local results, and a homeowner scrolling through profiles can tell the difference between stock photography and an actual job you completed.

Skip: stock photos of generic tools, generic houses, or smiling models in hard hats.

Use: photos of your own completed jobs, your crew on-site, and before/after shots when the work allows for it.

Reviews and Responses

Reviews are one of the biggest trust signals on the entire profile. Google counts them toward how prominent your business is, and replying to them is one of its listed tips.

  • Ask for a review shortly after a job wraps up, while the experience is still fresh
  • Respond to positive reviews with a short, genuine thank you, not a copy-pasted script
  • Respond to negative reviews calmly and professionally. How you handle a bad review often matters more to future customers than the review itself

Keeping It Updated

A profile that hasn't been touched in a year looks abandoned, even if your business is thriving. Keep hours accurate, especially around holidays, and keep your phone number current if it ever changes.

Posts and updates can help, but use them sparingly and with a purpose, a completed project, a seasonal service reminder, not filler content posted just to post something. A profile that's accurate and occasionally updated will outperform one that's either stale or spammed with noise.

What Gets Profiles Suspended

Most suspensions come from trying to look bigger or closer than you are. From Google's guidelines:

  • Keywords, cities, or taglines added to the business name
  • An address where you don't actually operate, including virtual offices and P.O. boxes
  • Showing an address that has no permanent sign and no staff during your stated hours
  • More than one profile for the same business

If your business is not appearing at all, start with why a contractor might not show up on Google Maps.
