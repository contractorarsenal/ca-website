What Homeowners Actually Look For Before They Call

Before anyone dials your number, they're quietly answering a handful of questions in their head. Your website should answer them first.

What Do You Do?

Before anything else, a homeowner needs to know your trade and your specific services match their problem. This has to be obvious in seconds, not buried in a paragraph about your company history.

Do You Work in My Area?

A homeowner in Everett doesn't want to call a company that's based in Seattle and might not travel that far. If your service area isn't clear, some people will assume the answer is no and move on.

Can I Trust You?

This is the biggest hurdle for anyone letting a stranger into their home or handing over a few thousand dollars. Trust gets built through a combination of the next few points, not any single one alone.

Real Project Examples

Photos of actual completed work tell a homeowner more than any amount of copy. Stock photos do the opposite. If it doesn't look like your work, it reads as generic, even if the writing is good.

What Do Reviews Say?

Reviews are one of the fastest trust signals a homeowner checks. A handful of specific, recent reviews matter more than a big number with no detail behind it.

Are You Licensed and Insured?

Where your trade requires licensing or insurance, say so clearly. It answers a real concern before it's even asked, and its absence raises a question homeowners notice.

Can I Contact You Easily?

A visible phone number and a simple way to request an estimate. If a homeowner has to search for how to reach you, some percentage of them simply won't bother.

Does the Company Look Active?

An outdated site, broken links, or a "coming soon" page makes a homeowner wonder if you're still in business. Recent project photos or a current year in the footer both help signal that someone is home.

What Happens After I Submit?

If someone fills out a form, tell them what to expect: a callback within a day, a text confirmation, anything. Silence after submitting a form is one of the fastest ways to lose a lead you already had.

None of this is complicated. It's the same list a homeowner would run through meeting a contractor in person: what do you do, do you work here, can I trust you, and how do I reach you. Your website just needs to answer it before they ever pick up the phone.
