Why Your Contractor Business Isn't Showing Up on Google Maps

There are two different problems hiding in that sentence. One is that your business isn't on Maps at all. The other is that it's there, but customers never see it. The fixes are not the same.

Figure Out Which Problem You Have

Open Google Maps and search for your exact business name plus your city.

  • Nothing comes up: your profile is missing, unverified, or suspended. Start with the next three sections
  • You come up by name, but not for "roofer near me" or "painter in Bothell": the profile exists and the issue is how you rank. Skip to the service area section and read from there

Do the second test from somewhere other than your own office, or ask a customer across town to try it. What you see from your own location is tilted toward you.

The Profile Was Never Verified

This is the most common reason a contractor is missing entirely. Someone started the profile, the verification step never got finished, and it has been sitting there since.

Sign in to the Google account that manages the business and look for a prompt to get verified. Google chooses which methods you are offered: a phone call or text, an email, a video recording, a live video call, or a postcard. Postcard codes usually arrive within 14 days, and Google's review after you submit can take up to five business days. While you wait for a postcard, don't change the business name, address, or category, and don't request another code. The steps are on Google's verification help page.

The Profile Is Suspended or Not Eligible

If the profile used to show and then disappeared, check for a suspension notice in the account. Google suspends profiles that break its guidelines, and contractors usually trip the same few rules:

  • Extra words in the business name. "Smith Roofing" is a name. "Smith Roofing, Best Roof Repair Seattle" is not, and Google says that kind of addition can get a profile suspended
  • An address you don't work from. P.O. boxes and rented virtual offices are not eligible
  • A visible address with no sign and no staff. Google only allows a shown address when the location has permanent signage and is staffed during your stated hours

Fix the cause first, then ask Google to reinstate the profile. Asking before you fix it tends to go nowhere.

There Is More Than One Profile

Google allows one profile per business, and says duplicates cause problems with how your information shows on Maps and Search. Duplicates happen more than you'd think: an old address, a profile a former employee made, one a marketing company set up years ago. Search your name, your phone number, and any old business names. If you find extras, claim them or report them rather than leaving them up.

Your Service Area Is Set Up Wrong

If you go to the customer, Google treats you as a service-area business. The correct setup, per Google's service-area page:

  • Remove your address from the profile if customers don't come to it. Showing a home address to try to look more local goes against the guidelines
  • Set your service areas by city or postal code. A radius is not an option
  • You can list up to 20 areas
  • Keep the whole area within about two hours of driving from where you are based
  • One profile covers the whole area. You can't open a profile for each city you'd like to work in

Listing a city as a service area tells Google you work there. It does not promise you'll show up for searches made there, which brings up distance.

The Searcher Is Too Far Away

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance, and prominence. Distance means how far the business is from the person searching. A contractor based in Everett will generally have a harder time appearing for someone searching from south Seattle than for someone in Everett, however good the profile is.

You can't change where your customers are. What helps in the towns farther out is everything else on this list: an exact category, strong reviews, and a website with real pages about the services and areas you cover.

Google Can't Tell What You Do

Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Check three things:

  • Primary category. Pick the most specific one that is true. A painter listed only as "Contractor" is a weaker match for "house painter" than one listed as "Painter"
  • Services. Fill in the services list with the jobs you actually want calls for
  • Your website. If the profile says you do cabinet refinishing and your site never mentions it, the two disagree. Each real service should have its own page

There Isn't Enough to Go On Yet

Prominence is how well known Google thinks you are. It names two inputs: reviews, and how many other websites link to your business. A brand-new profile with two reviews and no website is up against companies with years of history.

There is no shortcut, but there is a routine. Ask every happy customer for a review, reply to each one, add photos of finished jobs, and get your business listed in the places a real local contractor would be: your suppliers, your trade association, your manufacturer certifications.

Fix It in This Order

  • Find every profile for your business and get down to one
  • Get that one verified
  • Clean up the name so it is only your real business name
  • Set the address and service areas the way Google asks for your kind of business
  • Choose the most specific true primary category and fill in services
  • Make your website say the same things the profile does
  • Start a steady review habit

Google is direct about one thing: "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google." Nobody can promise you a spot on the map. What you can do is remove every reason for Google to leave you off it.
